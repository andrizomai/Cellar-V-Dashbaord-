# Presentation script — Cellar V Business Health Dashboard

Target length: **8 minutes** (assignment allows 5–10). Read this as a guide, not a
script to recite word-for-word — talk it, don't read it.

---

## 0:00–0:30 — Open (required elements)

- State your name.
- Hold your government-issued ID up to the camera, clearly legible, for a few seconds.
- One line on the setup: *"I'm going to walk through a business case for Cellar V,
  a wine bar and retail shop I run in Singapore, and a dashboard I built for its
  owner and investors to support a real decision about where to focus next."*

## 0:30–1:15 — The business case (stakeholders + decision)

- Stakeholders: **Cellar V's owner and its investors** — not a hypothetical.
- Decision: **where the business should prioritize its limited time and capital
  next** — tightening how it runs in person, growing membership, or fixing the
  online store.
- Say this plainly: *"I pulled real data from two systems — our point-of-sale
  system for the physical wine bar, and our Shopify store for online sales — to
  see what the numbers actually support, rather than assume."*

## 1:15–2:15 — Walk the dashboard: The Full Picture

- Share your screen, open the dashboard, land on **The Full Picture** tab.
- Call out the four KPI tiles: in-person sales (SGD 105,770), gross profit
  (SGD 62,859), transactions (807), guests served (1,763) — all over 7.5 months.
- Point at the two side-by-side boxes: in-person vs. online.
- Land the reframe in one sentence: *"The online store's entire three-year
  history doesn't add up to half of what the wine bar takes in on a single
  average day. This isn't a business in trouble — it's a business whose website
  hasn't caught up with it."*

## 2:15–3:00 — Walk the dashboard: Where the Money Comes From

- Switch tabs. Call out: Red wine is 61% of gross sales, more than every other
  category combined; the top 10 products — almost all Italian and French reds —
  already account for a quarter of all sales.
- Land the insight: *"This tells us who we are as a business before we decide
  what to fix — a red-wine-led, in-person wine bar, not a general retailer."*

## 3:00–4:00 — Walk the dashboard: Margin & Membership

- Switch tabs. This is the heart of the recommendation, so slow down here.
- Call out: 19.5% of gross sales — SGD 23,580 — given away in discounts, with
  Liquor and Champagne/Sparkling discounted hardest.
- Call out: members spend 2.2x more per item than non-members, but only 16
  people joined as members in 7.5 months.
- Land the insight: *"Here's where the actual opportunity is — not on the
  website, but in how disciplined we are about discounting, and how actively
  we're converting regulars into members."*

## 4:00–4:45 — Walk the dashboard: Online Channel

- Keep this brief — it's a real but smaller finding.
- Call out: 7 of 27 "Best seller"-tagged products are out of stock online; only
  25% of registered accounts have ever purchased.
- Land it: *"This is a cheap, easy fix. It's just not where the leverage is,
  which is why it comes after the in-person findings, not before them."*

## 4:45–6:00 — Course concepts you applied (rubric explicitly grades this)

Name 3–4 concepts and say *what choice each one drove*, e.g.:

- **User-centric design** — the dashboard is organized around the *decision*
  the owner and investors actually need to make, not around "here's every
  metric I could pull." That's why in-person performance comes before the
  online channel, even though the online data came first chronologically in my
  own analysis — the narrative order follows the size of the opportunity, not
  the order I found things in.
- **Storytelling with data (Knaflic)** — I open with a reframe ("the business
  is healthy, it's the website that's behind"), not a methodology slide,
  because that's the one sentence a busy investor needs before anything else
  lands.
- **Reducing clutter, choosing the right visual (Few, Bertin)** — I avoided
  bright alert-style color coding and decorative icons in favor of plain
  bordered callouts, since this is being read by an owner and investors, not
  browsed casually — the color that *does* appear (green/red for in-stock vs.
  out-of-stock) is reserved for the one place it's actually a meaningful status
  signal, not decoration.
- **Summarization over raw detail** — the KPI tiles show totals and rates, not
  a 300-line product ledger; the full detail exists underneath for anyone who
  wants to drill in, but the story leads with what the decision actually
  depends on.

## 6:00–7:00 — Recommendation

- Read directly off the **Recommendation** tab: what (discount discipline on
  Liquor and Champagne/Sparkling, an active membership ask at checkout,
  restocking the online best-sellers as a secondary step), who (the owner,
  day to day — this is an operating habit, not a project), and the metric to
  watch (category discount rate, new members per month).
- Say the investor-facing number out loud: *"A modest five-point tightening of
  the discount rate would recover on the order of six thousand dollars over a
  comparable period — several times the online channel's entire three-year
  revenue. That's why this is the priority, not the website."*

## 7:00–7:45 — Assumptions & limitations

- Pick the two that matter most to say out loud: this covers 7.5 months, not a
  full year, so the recovery figure is an illustration of scale, not a
  forecast; and "Custom Discount" is one undifferentiated bucket in the
  POS export, so the discount rate is a ceiling on discretionary discounting,
  not proof that any specific promotion was wrong.

## 7:45–8:00 — Close

- One sentence: *"Next step is tightening discounting on those two categories
  and making membership an active ask — and watching whether the discount rate
  and sign-up pace actually move."*
- Thank the viewer / sign off.

---

### Delivery notes
- Keep your face visible in a webcam window the whole time (screen + face, per
  the assignment requirement).
- Since the audience now explicitly includes investors, don't just read the
  numbers off the screen — say what each one means for a return-on-attention
  or return-on-capital decision, not just "what happened."
- If you go over ~9 minutes in a runthrough, cut from the course-concepts
  section first, and compress the Online Channel walkthrough — both are
  valuable but the most compressible without losing the core argument.
